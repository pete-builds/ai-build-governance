# Core Governance Model

**This layer binds you, once your institution adopts and amends it.** Thirteen
numbered chapters of requirements, plus a conventions page that tells you which
sentences bind and which explain.

**Read [How to read this](00-conventions.md) first.** It defines the normative
keywords, the statement labels, and the plain-language-to-construction
terminology mapping.

Requirements are numbered by chapter, so `11.2` is the second requirement of
chapter 11 and can be cited directly in an amendment or an exception record.
Numbers are stable across editions: a requirement is never renumbered, so a new
one is appended even where that puts it out of positional order.

Every chapter follows the same eight sections: Purpose, Failure this prevents,
Requirement, Applicability, Required evidence, Exceptions, Implementation
guidance, Sources and confidence.

**If you read only Purpose and Requirement across all thirteen, you have read
the model.**

## The chapters are not all the same kind of thing

Thirteen chapters in one numbered list reads as thirteen sequential stages, and
it is not.

**The model is one sentence.** Establish the need, classify the consequences, review the design, authorize construction, inspect at the right moments, approve production, and keep a record.

Everything else in it hangs off that sentence.

| | Step | Chapter | The failure it prevents |
|--:|:--|:--|:--|
| 1 | Establish the need | [Statement of Need](02-statement-of-need.md) | Building the wrong thing, solution-first |
| 2 | Classify the consequences | [Risk Classification](03-classification.md) | Risk decided by reviewer mood |
| 3 | Review the design | [Design Review](04-design-review.md) | A design nobody read against the requirement |
| 4 | Authorize construction | [Authorization to Build](05-authorization-to-build.md) | Approval becoming a permanent entitlement |
| 5 | Inspect at the right moments | [Inspections](06-inspections.md) | Verification after the evidence is gone |
| 6 | Approve production | [Production Approval and Records](07-production-approval.md) | Software arriving in production by being used more, with nobody having decided |
| 7 | Keep a record | [Production Approval and Records](07-production-approval.md) | Nobody being able to say what is running, what it can reach, and who owns it |
| ↺ | **When it changes, re-enter** | [Alterations and Existing Systems](08-alterations.md) | Change that silently outruns its review |

**Six chapters are not steps.** They are standing arrangements, true the
whole way through:

| Chapter | Applies |
|:--|:--|
| [Principles](01-principles.md) | Always. Where a chapter conflicts with a principle, the principle wins. |
| [Roles and Authority](09-roles.md) | From before the Statement of Need until decommissioning |
| [Concurrent Reviews](10-concurrent-reviews.md) | Alongside design review, never as a queue behind it |
| [Certified Reusable Components](11-certified-components.md) | Both the **entry point** for anything reusing existing work and an output of finishing |
| [Delegated Authority](12-delegated-authority.md) | Whenever an agent acts on a human's behalf |
| [Third-Party Capabilities](13-third-party.md) | Whenever a build depends on something the institution does not operate |

**Start at [Certified Reusable Components](11-certified-components.md), not at step 1.**
A capability consuming an already certified component inherits its inspection
and reviews only the integration. That is the fastest path through all of this,
and the only one that gets faster as an institution governs more.
